# Green heat incentives by country — 7 August 2026

**Scope expanded this run** to EU, India, China, Brazil, Pakistan, South East Asia,
Turkey and the Global multilateral layer, with UK, Ireland and US coverage retained.
Priority technologies are heat pumps (**HP**), biomass (**BIO**), biogas (**BGS**) and
heat storage (**STOR**); other low-carbon heat routes are tagged **OTHER** and still
reported.

**Targeting mode:** default-coverage. `agents/green-heat-incentives/supplier-sites.csv`
is empty, so the countries below are the run book's standing scope, **not Haleon's actual
supplier footprint**. No supplier names or locations have been invented.

**Confidence:** every figure below is sourced from web search, not read off the
administering body's own page — the environment blocks `www.gov.uk`,
`assets.publishing.service.gov.uk`, `www.gov.scot` and `businesswales.gov.wales`, and the
non-UK bodies were reached only via search summaries. Treat all figures as
**unconfirmed**. In the emerging-market geographies especially, headline numbers are
usually total programme envelopes rather than money a supplier can draw.

## 3. Global layer — multilateral finance

The most important structural point for the new geographies: in India, Brazil, Pakistan,
South East Asia and Turkey, the money that actually reaches a mid-sized industrial
supplier usually arrives as **concessional credit, guarantees or interest rebates through
a local bank** — not as a grant the supplier applies for. The right first question is
therefore "which participating financial institution serves this supplier", not "which
fund do we apply to".

funding.

**Read on India:** the biomass gasifier thermal subsidy is the most directly relevant
instrument found anywhere in the emerging-market set — it is explicitly for *process heat*,
not power generation, which is the distinction that usually rules these schemes out. At
roughly Rs 2 lakh per 300 kWth it is modest against total project cost, but it is a real
capital contribution with a defined rate. The catch is timing: National Bioenergy
Programme Phase-I ran to FY2025-26, so the successor phase must be confirmed before this
is raised with a supplier.

for Haleon suppliers than a grant would be; its value is in reducing the cost of the JCI
arrangement or funding the portion JCI does not cover.

**Read on Pakistan: this is the thinnest geography in the set, and honestly so.** The
research found policy frameworks, large biomass *potential* (840m tonnes of animal manure,
108m tonnes of crop residues annually) and precedent projects at sugar mills — but no
identifiable capital grant an industrial supplier could apply for. State Bank of Pakistan
concessionary refinancing has historically existed for renewable energy and should be
checked directly next run; it could not be confirmed here. For now the realistic lever is
import duty exemption on equipment, plus multilateral credit lines through local banks.

## 6. What this means for the programme

**The technology that attracts money varies by geography, and it is not heat pumps
everywhere.** Europe funds heat pumps and electrification; India, Brazil and Pakistan fund
biomass and biogas, because that is what their feedstock base supports. A supplier
conversation in São Paulo or Pune should open with biogas, not with the heat pump case
that works in Rotterdam. Heat storage barely appears as a funded category anywhere — it is
almost always bundled inside a broader efficiency or renewable-heat scheme rather than
supported in its own right.

**Outside Europe, the instrument type changes from grant to credit.** Guarantees, interest
rebates and concessional loans through local banks dominate. That interacts directly with
the JCI proposition: where JCI already removes the capex barrier, subsidised debt adds less
than a grant would. The strongest combinations are where a real grant or tax exemption
stacks on top — Ireland, France, Malaysia's guarantee-plus-rebate, and Turkey's customs and
VAT exemption on imported plant.

**Two headline numbers need handling with care.** China's 20% central subsidy excludes
pharma and packaging sectors entirely, and the JETP envelopes are power-sector-weighted and
running behind schedule. Both would embarrass the programme if quoted to a supplier
unchecked.
