# Green heat incentives — 4 August 2026

**Run mode:** jurisdiction-default. `agents/green-heat-incentives/supplier-sites.csv`
is empty, so county targeting is switched off and this baseline covers the UK national
and devolved layer plus a sample of the county layer to show what the targeted output
looks like. No supplier names or locations have been invented. Drop the supplier sites
into that CSV and the next run keys every section to the counties where Haleon
suppliers actually run heat assets.

**Baseline run:** no prior snapshots existed, so everything below is reported as new
rather than as week-on-week change. From the next run only changes are surfaced.

**Confidence:** the environment's network policy refused direct connections to
`www.gov.uk`, `assets.publishing.service.gov.uk`, `www.gov.scot` and
`businesswales.gov.wales`, so nothing here was read off the administering body's own
page. Every figure is sourced from web search and is marked **unconfirmed** until
verified. Confirm any deadline or grant ceiling before it goes in front of a supplier.

## 1. Closing soon (inside 90 days)

Two clocks are running, and the second one is the more consequential.

### Green Heat Network Fund, Round 12 — closes 25 September 2026 *(unconfirmed)*

- **Body:** DESNZ. England and Wales.
- **Funds:** capital grants for commercialisation and construction of new low-carbon
  heat networks, and retrofit or expansion of existing ones. Open to public, private and
  third-sector organisations. Funding drawdown runs to FY2029–30 *(unconfirmed)*.
- **Fit:** relevant where a supplier site sits close enough to an existing or planned
  network to take heat off it, or where a large site could host a source. This is a
  developer-scale instrument — a supplier will rarely lead a bid, but a supplier can be
  the anchor offtaker in someone else's, which is the realistic route in.
- **Next step:** for any supplier site in a town with a live heat network zone, ask the
  zone coordinator whether Round 12 or a later round could carry a connection.
- **Link:** https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/green-heat-network-fund-ghnf-guidance-on-how-to-apply

### The county-level layer closes on 30 September 2026 — and mostly does not come back

This is the finding that matters most in this baseline. The UK Shared Prosperity Fund,
which financed most county and district business decarbonisation grants, **ended in
March 2026**. Councils holding underspend are pushing it out now against a completion
deadline of **30 September 2026** *(unconfirmed)*. Practically:

- Grants still live are the tail of a closing programme, not a standing offer.
- "Completion by 30 September" usually means the work must be **done and invoiced**, not
  merely approved — so a supplier starting an application in September is already too
  late for anything requiring installation.
- After that date the county layer thins out sharply until whatever succeeds UKSPF is
  stood up. There is no confirmed successor.

The window for the local layer is therefore about eight weeks, and it is the layer with
the smallest grants but the lowest barrier — exactly the tier that gets a mid-sized
supplier to say yes to a first project.

**Recommended handling:** treat 30 September as a programme deadline for the Green Heat
comms plan, not a background fact. Any supplier already in conversation should be told
now, by their category manager, that local money exists and expires.

## 2. By county

County targeting is off in this run. The schemes below surfaced during research and are
included as **illustrations of the county layer's shape** — they are not the result of
matching against Haleon's supplier base, and none should be assumed relevant until the
site list is loaded.

| Scheme | Geography | Shape | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Net Zero Growth Grant | Bolsover, Derbyshire | Up to £25,000 for decarbonisation projects, low-carbon technology adoption, retrofit | Live, ends 30 Sep 2026 *(unconfirmed)* |
| Business Sustainability Programme | York & North Yorkshire | Free decarbonisation diagnostic visit plus capital grants c. £500–£14,000 | Live *(unconfirmed)* |
| Business Decarbonisation Grant 2025/26 | Nottingham City | Capital grant, applicant guidance published | Live *(unconfirmed)* |
| Business Decarbonisation & Green Economy Development Loan | Surrey | Loan rather than grant — subsidised finance | Live *(unconfirmed)* |
| Local Growth Fund Low Carbon Project | Sheffield | Capital support for low-carbon projects | Live *(unconfirmed)* |

What the pattern tells you, and what will hold for whichever counties your suppliers are
actually in:

- **Free diagnostic visits are the highest-value item here.** They cost the supplier
  nothing, produce the heat-demand data the value-at-stake one-pager needs, and pair
  naturally with a JCI desktop audit. Lead with these, not with capital grants.
- **Grant sizes are small** — £5k to £25k is typical. That will not fund a heat pump
  retrofit on its own. Its use is de-risking the feasibility stage and giving the
  supplier's commercial owner a reason to engage.
- **SME restrictions are near-universal**, and many of Haleon's heat-intensive suppliers
  will fail the headcount or turnover test. Check eligibility per site before raising it
  with a supplier; a withdrawn offer damages the programme's credibility more than never
  mentioning it.
- **Loans appear where grants have run out** (Surrey). Worth carrying, because with JCI
  removing capex the subsidised-finance route is largely redundant — a useful thing for a
  category manager to be able to say.

## 3. National and devolved layer

### Industrial Energy Transformation Fund — closed to new applications

The obvious first place to look is no longer available. DESNZ confirmed on 3 July 2025
that the IETF would not be extended and that the planned second Phase 3 competition
window would not take place *(unconfirmed)*. Existing Phase 1, 2 and first-Phase-3-window
projects continue to completion, backed by the £163m committed at Autumn Budget 2024.
DESNZ published an IETF evaluation on 29 January 2026 which reported that beneficiaries
found the fund attractive and that it supported deployment of mature industrial
decarbonisation technology at scale.

**Why this matters to the programme:** the flagship instrument for exactly this problem —
fuel switching in industrial process heat — is shut. Anyone who told a supplier in the
last two years to "look at IETF" was giving advice that has since expired. There is
currently no national capital grant of comparable scale open to a supplier wanting to
replace a gas steam boiler in England or Wales.

That absence is the strongest available argument for the JCI heat-as-a-service route.
The pitch is not "here is a grant" but "public capital for this has gone, and the
no-capex route is now the realistic one" — which is a more honest and more durable
message than grant-chasing.

**Watch item:** a successor described as an "Industrial Support Scheme", with
consultation during 2026 and launch in 2027, appears on commercial advisory sites but
**could not be corroborated from any government source**. Treat as rumour until DESNZ
confirms it. If real, the 2026 consultation is a place for Haleon to put a view, given
its supplier base.

### Scotland

- **Scottish Industrial Energy Transformation Fund (SIETF)** — the devolved equivalent,
  administered by the Scottish Government, and a separate route for sites in Scotland
  where the England/Wales IETF closure does not apply. Status of the current window
  unconfirmed. https://www.gov.scot/policies/energy-efficiency/scottish-industrial-energy-transformation-fund/
- **Scotland's Heat Network Fund** — capital grants to public and private sector, with
  £300m reported over the current parliamentary session, no fixed application deadline,
  and full drawdown required by March 2030 *(unconfirmed)*.

Scotland is currently the more favourable jurisdiction for a supplier seeking public
capital for heat. If Haleon has heat-intensive suppliers with Scottish sites, they should
be prioritised.

### Wales

Covered by the Green Heat Network Fund, which was extended to Wales. Business Wales
carries the wider business support offer; specific live heat schemes not confirmed in
this run.

### Northern Ireland

Nothing confirmed. Invest NI and the Department for the Economy are the right doors and
need checking directly on the next run — this is a gap in this baseline, not evidence

## What this baseline changes about the Green Heat programme

The comms plan assumed two levers, capital (JCI) and commercial pull (carbon pricing in
tenders). This research says something useful about a possible third:

**Public grants are not a third lever at national level in England and Wales — that door
is shut.** They are a real but small and expiring lever at county level, and a genuine
lever in Scotland. So grants should not be positioned as a headline in supplier
messaging. Their honest use is as a *door-opener at the feasibility stage*: a free
council diagnostic visit costs the supplier nothing, generates the heat data the
value-at-stake one-pager depends on, and gives the category manager a concrete,
time-limited reason to raise the subject in the next QBR rather than "sometime".

The IETF closure also sharpens the core message. With no national grant available, the
JCI no-capex route stops being one option among several and becomes the answer to
"how would we ever pay for this" — which is the objection the site owner actually has.
