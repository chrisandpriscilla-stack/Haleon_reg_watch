## 3. Country tables

### EU-wide layer — the most significant instrument available

This cuts across every EEA country and is, for a large heat-intensive supplier, the
biggest single pot on this list. It deserves to be read before the national tables.

| Scheme (body) | What it incentivises | What it can be used for | Value | Eligibility | How to apply | Deadline | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Innovation Fund — IF26 Heat Auction** (DG CLIMA / CINEA) | Switching industrial process heat from fossil fuel to electrified and renewable heat | Electrified and renewable heat technologies in energy-intensive industry — the pilot round covered pulp and paper, glass, steel and similar | **€1bn** budget from EU ETS revenues | Industrial operators across the EEA | Competitive auction — bidders compete on subsidy requested per tonne CO2e avoided, via the CINEA portal. Draft T&Cs published 22 May 2026; final T&Cs due **September 2026** | Opens **December 2026**, submission expected **February 2027** | Unconfirmed (EC/CINEA pages via search) |
| **Innovation Fund — IF25 Heat Auction** (pilot, now closed) | As above | As above | **€400m** awarded to **65 projects** across 10 countries, from 85 bids requesting €1.4bn | — | Closed 19 Feb 2026; grant agreements expected Q4 2026 | Closed | Unconfirmed |
| **Auctions-as-a-service top-ups** | National co-funding channelled through the same auction | As above | Germany **€1.3bn**, Spain **€490m** national funds on top of the EU pot | Operators in those countries | Same auction process | Tied to auction rounds | Unconfirmed |

**Why this is the headline.** The IF25 pilot was heavily oversubscribed — €1.4bn of bids
chasing €400m — and the Commission responded by confirming a second round at more than
double the size. Recipient countries in the pilot were Austria, Belgium, Czechia,
Denmark, France, Germany, Hungary, Portugal, Slovenia and Spain. **Notably absent: Poland
and Italy**, which is worth knowing if suppliers sit there.

The practical point for the Green Heat programme is the timing. Final terms land in
September, bidding opens in December, and bids close around February 2027. A supplier
that has not scoped a project by October will not bid. That makes the next eight weeks
the window for JCI desktop audits at any large European supplier site — the audit output
is exactly what a bid needs.

**Read on the UK:** the flagship instrument for exactly this problem is shut, and there is
currently no national capital grant of comparable scale open to a supplier replacing a gas
steam boiler in England or Wales. A successor described as an "Industrial Support Scheme"
launching 2027 appears on several consultancy sites but **could not be corroborated from
any government source** — treat as rumour. Scotland is the more favourable UK jurisdiction
today. The local layer is the tail of a closing programme, not a standing offer.

worth modelling properly for any large German supplier site.

**Read on the Netherlands:** the best-designed stack here. SDE++ covers operating cost,
EIA and MIA/Vamil reduce the tax cost of the capital, and ISDE handles smaller units. A
Dutch supplier can combine instruments in a way that is not possible in most other
countries. The SDE++ window is narrow and fixed — four weeks from 27 October — so it needs
diarising now.

### United States

**No federal industrial process-heat grant confirmed this run.** Searches returned
predominantly residential and buildings-focused material — Section 25C, 179D, and state
heat pump rebate programmes such as Colorado's and TECH Clean California — none of which
addresses industrial process heat. Section 48C was not confirmed as available. The federal
policy position has been in flux, so this needs a dedicated pass against DOE and IRS
sources rather than an inference from search summaries.

The sub-national layer is where US support for this is most likely to sit, and it is
state-by-state. Once the site list carries US states in `region_or_county`, that becomes
tractable; without it, a blanket fifty-state sweep is not a good use of a weekly run.

### India

| **PAT — Perform, Achieve, Trade** (BEE) | Beating an assigned energy-intensity target | Tradable Energy Saving Certificates (ESCerts) for over-performance; under-performers must buy | Market-priced ESCerts on the Indian Energy Exchange | Designated energy-intensive consumers | Compliance mechanism, not an application | **Being phased out** in favour of India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS) | Unconfirmed |

### China

**No capital subsidy confirmed.** The Top-1000 / Top-10000 energy-consuming enterprises

mandate, not money. Provincial-level support is more likely to carry actual funding and

## 5. What this means for the programme

Three things change the supplier message.

**The EU Heat Auction is the main event, and it has a clock.** Final terms in September,
bidding opens December, bids close around February 2027. It is the only instrument here
sized for a serious industrial heat conversion, and it was oversubscribed three-to-one
last round. Any large European supplier site should be scoping now, and the JCI desktop
audit is the natural way to generate what a bid needs.

**Grant availability is now a reason to differentiate between suppliers, not a blanket
message.** Ireland at 40%, France at up to 65%, Germany with a €6bn contracts-for-
difference pot and the Netherlands with a stackable tax-and-subsidy package are genuinely
attractive. The UK, post-IETF, is not. Sending every supplier the same "grants are
available" note would be wrong in most of these countries.

**The UK's IETF closure strengthens the JCI pitch rather than weakening it.** With no
national capital grant open, the no-capex route stops being one option among several and
becomes the answer to the objection the site owner actually has — "how would we ever pay
for this".
